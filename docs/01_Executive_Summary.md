# Executive Summary

**Prepared for:** Olist leadership (Sales, Marketing, Finance, Operations)
**Prepared by:** Mohammad Ammar — Data Analyst
**Period covered:** Full transaction history in the Olist public dataset (2016–2018)

## Headline

Olist generated **R$15.8M** in revenue across **95,420 orders**, at an average order value of **R$161**. Revenue grew steadily through mid-period before flattening and declining — a pattern consistent with seasonality rather than demand collapse.

## What we found

- **Concentration, not scarcity, is the main risk.** A small number of product categories and a small number of states account for the majority of revenue. That's efficient in a stable environment, but it means the business has limited insulation if any single category or region weakens.
- **The customer base is not compounding.** The large majority of customers purchase once and do not return. Revenue growth is therefore driven predominantly by new-customer acquisition — a more expensive and less durable engine than repeat purchasing.
- **Seasonality is real and predictable.** The mid-year peak and subsequent decline recurred in a pattern that inventory, staffing, and marketing planning can be built around rather than reacted to.

## What we recommend

| Horizon | Action |
|---|---|
| 0–3 months | Launch a repeat-purchase campaign targeting recent one-time buyers |
| 3–6 months | Build a category-diversification watchlist; pilot marketing spend in one under-penetrated state |
| 6–12 months | Institutionalize this dashboard as the standing monthly KPI review |

## Why this matters now

None of these are new markets, new products, or new capital. They are re-allocations of existing spend and attention toward the two structural weaknesses (retention, concentration) that currently cap growth durability. The supporting data, KPI definitions, and full methodology are documented in the rest of this repository for anyone who wants to verify or extend the analysis.
