# Business Problem

## What problems exist

Leadership has reliable top-line numbers (revenue, order count, AOV) but no structured, repeatable view of:

1. **Where** that revenue actually comes from at the category and regional level, and how concentrated it is.
2. **Who** is generating it — new customers or repeat customers — and in what proportion.
3. **When** demand predictably rises and falls, and whether current planning accounts for it.

Without this, category, regional, and retention decisions are being made from intuition and anecdote rather than from a shared, queryable source of truth.

## What decisions cannot currently be made

- Marketing cannot confidently decide **which categories or regions deserve incremental budget** without a concentration and growth-rate view.
- Customer Success cannot **size or justify a retention program** without knowing the actual repeat-purchase rate and how it compares to what "healthy" would look like for this business model.
- Finance cannot **forecast with confidence** without knowing how much of current revenue sits in a small number of categories/states that could underperform independently of overall demand.
- Operations cannot **plan staffing and inventory** around seasonality without a documented, recurring pattern rather than a one-time observation.

## What risks exist

| Risk | Description | Why it's urgent |
|---|---|---|
| Concentration risk | Majority of revenue tied to a small set of categories and states | A localized disruption has an outsized effect on total revenue |
| Retention risk | Majority of customers purchase once | Growth is dependent on continuously replacing the customer base, which is costlier than retaining it |
| Planning risk | Seasonal peak/trough is real but undocumented as a standing pattern | Inventory and staffing decisions risk being reactive rather than planned |

This document frames the "why" behind every KPI, query, and recommendation that follows in this repository.
