# Business Background

## Who is the company

Olist is a Brazilian marketplace-enablement platform. It does not manufacture or hold its own inventory; instead, it connects small and medium-sized independent sellers across Brazil to buyers through major online retail channels, handling the marketplace infrastructure (listings, order routing, logistics coordination, and customer communication) on the sellers' behalf.

## Industry

E-commerce marketplace / retail-enablement, operating within Brazil's broader online retail sector. This sits closer to a multi-tenant platform business (revenue depends on transaction volume across many independent merchants) than to a traditional single-brand retailer.

## How they operate

1. A seller lists products on the Olist platform.
2. A customer places an order, which may include items from one or more sellers.
3. Olist coordinates order fulfillment, logistics, and delivery tracking.
4. After delivery (or the estimated delivery date), the customer receives a satisfaction survey and can leave a review.

Because Olist's revenue is aggregated across many small sellers rather than concentrated in a few large accounts, its growth is structurally tied to **breadth** (how many categories and regions are active) and **repeatability** (whether buyers return) rather than to deepening a relationship with a small set of large customers, which is the more common growth lever for single-brand retailers.

## Why this problem matters

A marketplace business is exposed in ways a single-brand retailer often isn't:

- **Category concentration risk** — if the platform's revenue leans heavily on a handful of categories, a shift in demand for those categories (competition, price sensitivity, substitution) affects total revenue directly, since there's no single flagship product to fall back on.
- **Geographic concentration risk** — if a small number of states drive most revenue, local disruptions (logistics bottlenecks, regional economic conditions, competitive entry) have outsized impact.
- **Retention risk** — a platform that depends on constantly acquiring new one-time buyers has a structurally more expensive growth model than one retaining and re-engaging existing buyers, since acquisition costs recur every period while retention costs amortize.

This analysis exists to give leadership a data-grounded view of exactly how exposed the business currently is on each of these three dimensions, and what to do about it.
