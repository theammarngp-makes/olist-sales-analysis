# Business Insights

Each insight below follows the same structure: **finding → why it matters → what it implies**, rather than stopping at description.

## 1. Growth has a ceiling under current conditions

Revenue climbed steadily through mid-period, then plateaued and declined. Read alone, a decline looks like a demand problem. Read against the monthly pattern, it's consistent with seasonality — meaning the plateau is likely to recur on the same calendar cycle unless proactively planned around. **Implication:** treat the mid-year peak as a plannable event (inventory, staffing, marketing timing), not a one-off surprise.

## 2. The business is running a concentrated bet, not a diversified one

A small number of product categories account for the majority of revenue. In a stable environment that's an efficient allocation of attention — the platform is putting its weight behind what already works. But it also means the whole top line moves with a small number of categories. **Implication:** a category-diversification watchlist is not about "adding variety," it's about reducing single-point-of-failure exposure.

## 3. Geographic revenue is similarly concentrated

A small number of states drive a disproportionate share of revenue. This is a structural — not incidental — dependency: a logistics disruption, a competitive entrant, or a regional economic shift in the top state has a materially larger effect on total revenue than the same disruption in a smaller state would. **Implication:** regional diversification should be evaluated as a risk-hedging exercise, with success measured by exposure reduction, not just incremental revenue.

## 4. Retention, not acquisition, is the weakest link

The majority of customers purchase once and do not return. That means current revenue is substantially a function of continuously acquiring new customers — a growth engine that is more expensive and less durable than one built on repeat engagement, because acquisition cost recurs every period while retention cost amortizes over a customer's lifetime. **Implication:** of the three findings here, this is the one where a modest, low-cost intervention (targeted retention campaign) has the clearest, fastest line to durable impact — see `13_Business_Recommendations.md`.

## What these insights are not

None of these claim a specific dollar impact if addressed — see `13_Business_Recommendations.md` and `14_Limitations.md` for why no financial projection is invented here, and what would be required to responsibly produce one.
